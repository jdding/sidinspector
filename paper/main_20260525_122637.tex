\begin{abstract}
Semantic-ID (\sid) tokenizers are increasingly released as item-to-code
mappings that define the address space for generative recommenders. Yet public
mappings rarely share an inspection interface: before training a generator,
users must separately check catalog coverage, full-code aliasing,
prefix--co-occurrence alignment, tail capacity, and prefix fan-out. We present
\tool, a mapping-first diagnostic resource for \sid tokenizer artifacts. \tool
defines a small adapter contract over item mappings, metadata, interactions,
and optional traces; validates the contract; and reports
mapping-level probes for utilization, aliasing, neighborhood alignment,
popularity allocation, and structural cost, with hooks for temporal churn and
trace-based checks.

\tool produces inspectable profiles, not a leaderboard. On a 23,742-item
Musical worked example, a controlled GRID/RQ-KMeans-style feature-text export
has 3,749 unique full codes and a 0.977 full-code aliasing rate, while a
bounded ReSID/GAOQ export is aliasing-free in its exported mapping. The
strongest prefix--co-occurrence alignment, however, comes from a deterministic
category-prefix control rather than either learned export row (0.447 versus
0.154 and 0.055--0.080). This separates addressability from behaviorally
meaningful prefixes. Auxiliary checks replicate the diagnostic direction on
All\_Beauty, add a real Sports GRID export with complete joins, and show that
D3 is positively associated with candidate recall under a fixed train-only
reranker. Mechanism probes further separate raw aliasing volume,
interaction-qualified aliasing risk, head-to-tail capacity, and realized prefix
cost. \tool is not a new tokenizer or a benchmark suite; it makes \sid
tokenizer exports auditable before downstream generator training.
\end{abstract}
