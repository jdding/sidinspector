## Landscape Summary

Public OPE has a clean estimator/tooling anchor in Open Bandit Dataset / Open Bandit Pipeline (OBP): OBP explicitly exposes logged propensities and implements common OPE estimators including DM, IPW, SNIPW, DR, switch estimators, DRos, MRDR, and DML. This is the safest sanity baseline, but its lifecycle semantics are weak.

KuaiRand is the strongest public sequential-recommendation candidate because it contains randomly exposed videos inserted into normal recommendation feeds, user IDs, timestamps, rich user/item side information, multiple feedback signals, and scenario/policy metadata. The central unresolved gate is whether its random-exposure subset and policy metadata are sufficient for a defensible state-level OPE protocol rather than just observational diagnostics.

KuaiRec is not a logged-policy OPE dataset in the same sense, but the near fully observed user-item matrix makes it valuable for oracle-style stress tests: we can create controlled missingness, sparse-support regimes, and target-policy shifts, then compare estimated value to a known or near-known value.

MIND is useful for temporal and impression-scale diagnostics because it contains user histories plus clicked and non-clicked impressions, but public MIND does not expose known logging propensities. Therefore it should be labeled D0 or at most D1 with estimated propensities; it must not be used to make strict IPS/DR claims.

Closest method/literature anchors are slate OPE (Swaminathan et al., NeurIPS 2017), cascade DR for ranking policies (Kiyohara et al., WSDM 2022), counterfactual LTR with propensity scoring, deficient-support OPE, and lifecycle recommendation such as STAN. The gap worth attacking is not "lifecycle improves recommendation", but "lifecycle transitions create structured positivity/support failures that aggregate OPE can hide."

## Recommended Ideas

### Idea 1: Lifecycle-State Credibility Protocol for OPE

1. **Title**: Lifecycle-State Credibility Protocol for OPE
2. **One-sentence summary**: Build a public benchmark protocol that labels OPE estimates as credible, weakly credible, or observational-only separately for onboarding, active, pre-churn, and return-like states.
3. **Core hypothesis**: Aggregate OPE can pass conventional diagnostics while one or more lifecycle states have poor support, inflated variance, or unstable value rankings.
4. **Minimum viable public-data experiment**: Use OBP to verify estimator implementation; define lifecycle-like states on KuaiRand from pre-exposure history length, inactivity gap, recent engagement decay, and exposure frequency; compute state-level support violation rate, effective sample size, weight-tail metrics, confidence interval inflation, and aggregate-vs-state disagreement; use KuaiRec for an oracle-style stress test under controlled missingness.
5. **Closest prior work and novelty delta**: OBP provides estimators and realistic logged bandit data; slate/ranking OPE studies ranking estimators; STAN models lifecycle for multi-task recommendation. The delta is a lifecycle-centered reliability protocol for OPE credibility, not a new recommender model.
6. **Expected contribution type**: diagnostic / protocol / resource.
7. **Risk level and estimated effort**: Medium; 2-4 weeks for a credible public pilot if KuaiRand schema supports the required pre-exposure state definitions.
8. **Likely reviewer objection**: "This is a diagnostic benchmark, not a new estimator; lifecycle states may be hand-engineered and dataset-specific."

### Idea 2: Lifecycle-Conservative Policy Selection

1. **Title**: Lifecycle-Conservative Policy Selection under Sparse Support
2. **One-sentence summary**: Select a target policy only when lifecycle-state lower confidence bounds and support diagnostics pass, instead of maximizing aggregate OPE value.
3. **Core hypothesis**: A policy-selection rule with state-level credibility gates will reject target policies that look best in aggregate but are unsafe or unsupported for sparse lifecycle cohorts.
4. **Minimum viable public-data experiment**: Train or define simple target policies on OBP/KuaiRand, such as popularity, recency, diversity, and engagement-weighted variants; compare aggregate-OPE selection against lifecycle-conservative selection using SNIPS/DR/switch estimators; evaluate regret or value loss on OBP ground truth and KuaiRec oracle-style stress tests.
5. **Closest prior work and novelty delta**: Conservative policy selection exists in contextual bandits, and OBP supports OPE estimator comparison. The delta is a sequential-recommendation policy-selection rule constrained by lifecycle-state credibility, not just aggregate confidence.
6. **Expected contribution type**: method / protocol.
7. **Risk level and estimated effort**: Medium-High; 3-6 weeks because the method must avoid looking like a trivial min-over-groups rule.
8. **Likely reviewer objection**: "This is group-robust selection with lifecycle labels; why is the lifecycle structure technically necessary?"

### Idea 3: Lifecycle-Targeted Exploration Budget Simulator

1. **Title**: Lifecycle-Targeted Exploration Budget for Auditable Sequential Recommendation
2. **One-sentence summary**: Simulate how a small amount of randomized exposure allocated to sparse lifecycle states improves future OPE reliability relative to uniform exploration.
3. **Core hypothesis**: The same exploration budget yields much larger OPE credibility gains when allocated to low-support lifecycle states than when spread uniformly over all traffic.
4. **Minimum viable public-data experiment**: Use KuaiRec as an oracle environment and KuaiRand random-exposure rows as a realism check; simulate adding 0.1%, 0.5%, 1%, and 2% exploration to onboarding/pre-churn/return-like states; measure ESS gain, confidence-width reduction, support-violation reduction, and policy-ranking stability under fixed user-cost budgets.
5. **Closest prior work and novelty delta**: Prior exploration/OPE work studies exploration and deficient support broadly. The delta is a deployable logging-design question: where should exploration be spent so lifecycle-stratified OPE becomes credible later?
6. **Expected contribution type**: protocol / method / resource.
7. **Risk level and estimated effort**: Medium; 3-5 weeks for simulations, longer if tying to real randomized rows in KuaiRand is messy.
8. **Likely reviewer objection**: "This is a simulation, not evidence that a production recommender should allocate exploration this way."

### Idea 4: State-Conditioned Positivity Stress Curves

1. **Title**: State-Conditioned Positivity Stress Curves
2. **One-sentence summary**: Create support-thinning curves that show how OPE error, variance, and coverage degrade separately by lifecycle state.
3. **Core hypothesis**: OPE estimators have predictable state-specific failure thresholds; a global overlap metric will miss the first failing state.
4. **Minimum viable public-data experiment**: On KuaiRec, construct full-information policy values, then remove exposures with state-dependent thinning; on KuaiRand, repeat with observed random-exposure slices where possible; plot estimator bias, RMSE, confidence coverage, ESS, and weight-tail percentiles across thinning levels.
5. **Closest prior work and novelty delta**: Deficient-support OPE already studies missing context-action support. The delta is a sequential-recommendation benchmark where deficient support is structured by lifecycle transitions rather than random missingness.
6. **Expected contribution type**: diagnostic / theory-lite.
7. **Risk level and estimated effort**: Low-Medium; 2-3 weeks.
8. **Likely reviewer objection**: "Controlled thinning is artificial; the result may restate known positivity problems."

### Idea 5: Aggregate-to-State OPE Reversal Benchmark

1. **Title**: Aggregate-to-State OPE Reversal Benchmark
2. **One-sentence summary**: Construct cases where aggregate OPE says a target policy wins, but lifecycle-state OPE reveals losses or unsupported estimates in critical states.
3. **Core hypothesis**: Sequential recommendation has Simpson-style evaluation reversals because state mix and support quality change together.
4. **Minimum viable public-data experiment**: Use KuaiRec to define oracle values for simple policies and then sample logged data with lifecycle-skewed exposure; verify whether aggregate IPS/SNIPS/DR rankings reverse when evaluated by state; check whether KuaiRand shows analogous aggregate-vs-state disagreement without claiming oracle truth.
5. **Closest prior work and novelty delta**: Slate OPE and cascade DR address action-space/ranking variance; fairness and group evaluation study subgroup performance. The delta is an OPE-specific reversal benchmark tied to lifecycle support, not demographic fairness.
6. **Expected contribution type**: diagnostic / resource.
7. **Risk level and estimated effort**: Medium; 2-4 weeks.
8. **Likely reviewer objection**: "The reversal examples may be cherry-picked unless the benchmark generation rules are pre-registered and broad."

### Idea 6: State-Adaptive Switch/Clipping Estimators

1. **Title**: State-Adaptive Switch and Clipping Estimators for Lifecycle OPE
2. **One-sentence summary**: Replace a single global clipping or switch threshold with thresholds chosen per lifecycle state from estimated MSE or support diagnostics.
3. **Core hypothesis**: A global clipping threshold either over-clips well-supported states or under-clips sparse states; state-adaptive thresholds reduce worst-state error without materially hurting aggregate value estimation.
4. **Minimum viable public-data experiment**: Implement state-adaptive clipped IPS, SNIPS, and switch-DR on OBP synthetic/semi-synthetic settings and KuaiRec stress tests; compare against global clipping/switch, DRos, and MRDR; report aggregate and worst-state RMSE/coverage.
5. **Closest prior work and novelty delta**: Switch estimators, DRos, and MRDR already optimize robustness. The delta is not clipping itself; it is using lifecycle-stratified support as the unit of estimator adaptation and reporting worst-state reliability.
6. **Expected contribution type**: method.
7. **Risk level and estimated effort**: Medium-High; 4-8 weeks.
8. **Likely reviewer objection**: "This may be a straightforward subgroup extension of existing shrinkage estimators unless backed by theory or strong empirical gains."

### Idea 7: Multi-Reward Lifecycle OPE Consistency

1. **Title**: Multi-Reward Lifecycle OPE Consistency Audit
2. **One-sentence summary**: Test whether state-level OPE conclusions remain consistent across click, watch time, like, follow, and negative-feedback rewards.
3. **Core hypothesis**: Sparse lifecycle states amplify reward-choice sensitivity; a policy may look credible for clicks but unsupported or harmful for longer-term engagement proxies.
4. **Minimum viable public-data experiment**: On KuaiRand, compute lifecycle-stratified OPE diagnostics for multiple feedback signals; compare policy ranking stability and support diagnostics per reward; label where reward-window or reward-type choice changes the credibility status.
5. **Closest prior work and novelty delta**: KuaiRand exposes rich feedback signals, and lifecycle multi-task models such as STAN show that objectives vary by stage. The delta is evaluating OPE reliability across rewards, not training a multi-task predictor.
6. **Expected contribution type**: diagnostic.
7. **Risk level and estimated effort**: Medium; 2-4 weeks.
8. **Likely reviewer objection**: "Reward disagreement is expected; the paper must show the disagreement is specifically an OPE reliability problem, not just multi-objective recommendation."

### Idea 8: Lifecycle Definition Identifiability Audit

1. **Title**: Lifecycle Definition Identifiability Audit for Public Logs
2. **One-sentence summary**: Compare lifecycle-state definitions and determine which can be computed without post-treatment leakage and still predict OPE support failure.
3. **Core hypothesis**: Simple pre-exposure definitions such as history length and inactivity gap are less expressive but more defensible than latent-stage definitions learned from future feedback.
4. **Minimum viable public-data experiment**: On KuaiRand and MIND, define states using only information available before the impression/exposure; compare history-length bins, inactivity gaps, recent engagement slope, and latent HMM/cluster variants; evaluate leakage risk, state stability, state size, ESS, and support-violation predictiveness.
5. **Closest prior work and novelty delta**: STAN learns latent lifecycle stages for prediction. The delta is identifiability and auditability of lifecycle definitions for OPE, where post-treatment leakage invalidates the evaluation.
6. **Expected contribution type**: protocol / resource.
7. **Risk level and estimated effort**: Low-Medium; 1-3 weeks.
8. **Likely reviewer objection**: "This is preprocessing methodology; it may not be publishable alone."

### Idea 9: Ranking/Slate Support Diagnostics by Lifecycle

1. **Title**: Lifecycle-Stratified Support Diagnostics for Ranking and Slate OPE
2. **One-sentence summary**: Extend lifecycle diagnostics from item-level actions to ranking prefixes, cascade exposures, and slate-level support.
3. **Core hypothesis**: Sparse lifecycle states fail earlier at the slate/prefix level than at the item level, so item-action overlap can overstate ranking-OPE credibility.
4. **Minimum viable public-data experiment**: First inspect KuaiRand/MIND schema for logged ranking or impression-list fields; if usable, compute prefix-level item support, position support, and cascade-style exposure diagnostics by lifecycle state; compare item-level IPS diagnostics with ranking-aware diagnostics. If propensities are missing, label the output D0/D1 only.
5. **Closest prior work and novelty delta**: Swaminathan et al. study slate OPE; Kiyohara et al. propose cascade DR for ranking policies. The delta is lifecycle-stratified support failure analysis, not a replacement for their estimators.
6. **Expected contribution type**: diagnostic / protocol.
7. **Risk level and estimated effort**: High; 4-8 weeks due to schema and propensity limitations.
8. **Likely reviewer objection**: "Without logged slate propensities, this cannot be strict ranking OPE."

### Idea 10: Public Log OPE Readiness Validator

1. **Title**: Public Log OPE Readiness Validator
2. **One-sentence summary**: Build a small validator that classifies logs into D0/D1/D2 readiness levels and emits lifecycle-OPE missing-field reports.
3. **Core hypothesis**: Many public sequential recommendation datasets are useful for diagnostics but fail strict OPE requirements because they lack propensities, candidate sets, policy versions, or reward-window provenance.
4. **Minimum viable public-data experiment**: Implement a metadata/schema validator and run it on OBP, KuaiRand, KuaiRec, MIND, Yahoo references, and Coat; produce a machine-readable readiness card covering propensities, candidate sets, logged scores, policy IDs, randomization, timestamps, user history, reward definitions, and lifecycle-state computability.
5. **Closest prior work and novelty delta**: OBP is a tooling benchmark for OPE; dataset papers document their own schemas. The delta is a cross-dataset readiness standard focused on lifecycle-stratified OPE and public-to-production transfer.
6. **Expected contribution type**: resource / protocol.
7. **Risk level and estimated effort**: Low; 1-3 weeks.
8. **Likely reviewer objection**: "This is a checklist/tool, not a research contribution unless paired with empirical failures from Ideas 1-5."

## Top 3 Recommended for Novelty Check

1. **Lifecycle-State Credibility Protocol for OPE**
   Best public-phase backbone. It is honest about the contribution being diagnostic/protocol, and it can absorb Ideas 4, 5, 8, and 10 as modules. Novelty check should search for "group/state-stratified OPE reliability", "lifecycle OPE recommendation", "subgroup support diagnostics recommender OPE", and "sequential recommendation OPE overlap diagnostics".

2. **Lifecycle-Conservative Policy Selection**
   Best chance to become a method contribution if the protocol alone feels too resource-track. Novelty check should focus on conservative policy selection, distributionally robust/group OPE selection, safe policy improvement under subgroup constraints, and whether lifecycle state adds anything beyond generic group robustness.

3. **Lifecycle-Targeted Exploration Budget Simulator**
   Best bridge from public preflight to later production validation because it converts diagnostics into logging/exploration requirements. Novelty check should search active data collection for OPE, deficient-support repair, targeted exploration for recommender logging, and exploration allocation under subgroup/temporal support constraints.

## Brutal Assessment

- **Most publishable public-only package**: Idea 1 + Idea 4 + Idea 5 + Idea 10, framed as a benchmark/protocol/resource paper. This is safer than pretending to have a new causal estimator.
- **Most likely top-venue method path**: Idea 2 or Idea 6, but only if novelty check finds that lifecycle-state constraints/adaptation are not already covered by group-robust OPE or conservative policy selection.
- **Most strategically useful for eventual production validation**: Idea 3, because it yields a concrete answer to "what randomized logging do we need before internal OPE is credible?"
- **Weakest standalone idea**: Idea 8; it is necessary infrastructure but too close to cohort engineering unless attached to a stronger OPE result.
- **Highest overclaim risk**: Idea 9; ranking/slate OPE sounds strong, but public datasets may not expose the slate propensities needed for strict claims.
